# Assumptions

Every value or modelling choice here is one that the identified circuit or a
TP measurement does not give. Each has its value and its reason.

| # | Module | Assumption | Value | Reason |
|---|--------|-----------|-------|--------|
| A1 | circuit | Two Γ layouts, selected by `MotorParams.layout`. **tp3** (default): V1 → R1 in series (carries I1) → Rf ∥ jLω → jN'2ω + R'2/g. **terminals**: Rf ∥ jXm across V1, in parallel with R1 + jX + R'2/g (R1 carries I'2). Both reduce to a Thévenin source seen by the rotor branch (tp3: V·Zm/(R1+Zm), R1∥Zm; terminals: V, R1), which gives g_c and T_max in closed form; `breakdown()` also finds them numerically | tp3 | The user decided on tp3 (TP3 Figure 1: TP3 subtracts 3·R1·I0² at no-load, so R1 carries I0) and asked to keep the terminals layout available. With R1 = 0 the two are identical: checked against `docs/TD No 5.pdf`, Ex 1 figure (230 Ω ∥ j46 Ω across Vs, then 2/g + j4), g_c = 0,5, T_max = 126,3 N·m. The TP3 motor handout itself is not available (`TP_M3_ENP_Final_Complet.pdf` is the diode-rectifier TP). |
| A2 | circuit | Under V/f the reactances scale with f/f_n; R1, R'2, Rf stay constant | — | Reactance = L·ω with L fixed by the circuit. Rf frequency dependence is not measured; refined in `losses.py` if TP data allow. |
| A3 | circuit | Stator copper loss: 3·R1·I1² (tp3) or 3·R1·I'2² (terminals). Iron loss 3·E²/Rf, with E = V − R1·I1 (tp3) or E = V (terminals) | — | Consequence of the layout (A1). On TD5-like data with R1 = 1 Ω the two layouts give T_max = 97,4 vs 98,6 N·m. |
| A4 | circuit | Copper zero-resistance temperature 234.5 °C, reference 20 °C | 234,5 °C / 20 °C | Standard annealed copper value (course formula). |
| A5 | identification | DC test measured between two line terminals of the star winding: R1 = U_dc / (2·I_dc) | — | Star connection convention (CLAUDE.md). A delta-connected motor needs its own formula. |
| A6 | identification | The no-load test is done at rated voltage and frequency; they become V and f of the identified MotorParams | — | Rf and Xm depend on the flux level; the no-load test sets that level. |
| A7 | identification | `dc=None` means R1 = 0 | 0 Ω | TD5 Ex 1 hypothesis R1 ≈ 0. Used only when there is no DC test. |
| A8 | identification | Mechanical losses constant near synchronous speed, iron losses ∝ U²: p_mec = intercept at U = 0 of P0' = P0 − 3·R1·I0² vs U0² | — | Classical TP3 separation. With R1 ≠ 0, P_fe ∝ E² (not U²) and the no-load slip adds rotor copper loss, so the intercept is slightly biased: 59,97 W found for 60 W true on the démo fixture. When the statement gives the losses (TD5 Ex 1: "negligible"), `TestRecords.p_mech` takes that value and no fit is done. |
| A9 | identification | "Classical" locked-rotor formulas neglect the magnetising branch: R'2 = Pcc/(3·Icc²) − R1, X = sqrt(Zcc² − Rcc²) | — | Course formula. Its own error on TD5 Ex 1 (noise-free): R'2 = 1,7375 Ω vs 2 Ω (−13,1 %), X = 3,6922 Ω vs 4 Ω (−7,7 %), hence g_c = 0,4706 (−5,9 %) and T_max = 136,82 N·m (+8,3 %). It is exactly Re/Im of the measured Zcc, because Zm (≈ 45 Ω) is only ~10× |Z2| (≈ 4,5 Ω) at g = 1. The classical no-load formulas (E ≈ V) add +0,48 % on Rf and Xm with R1 = 0,5 Ω. The "exact" method (default) removes both errors. |
| A10 | circuit | `efficiency(g)` uses the internal mechanical power (1 − g)·P_gap: mechanical losses are not in the circuit. Motor η = P_mec/P_in, generator η = P_in/P_mec, η = 0 when there is no useful output (no-load, locked rotor, plugging) | 0 W mechanical loss | TD5 Ex 1: "negligible mechanical losses". Shaft-side mechanical losses are added in `core/losses.py` (stage 4). |
| A11 | identification | The "exact" no-load and locked-rotor formulas invert the chosen layout; the "classical" formulas are the TP3 course recipe whatever the layout | — | In the terminals layout R1 carries no no-load current, so 3·R1·I0² is not subtracted. Identifying with the wrong layout gives a wrong circuit (tested). |
| A12 | identification | The no-load point used for Rf and Xm is the one closest to the nameplate voltage, and must be within 10 % of it | 10 % | Bench points are usually 10 % of Un apart. A 5 % tolerance rejected about 1 % of runs with 2 % voltmeter noise; 10 % is 5σ and still rejects a series with no rated point. |
| A13 | identification | Load-point fit (`fit_load_points`) fits only Xm, X, R'2; R1 is fixed from the DC test and Rf from the no-load test | — | **Identifiability.** On TD5 Ex 1 load points (g = 0,02…0,40), +1 % on a parameter moves the normalised residual vector by: Rf 0,0014, Xm 0,0047, X 0,0093, R'2 0,0104. With 2,5 % noise (‖noise‖ ≈ 0,15 over 36 residuals) Rf would have to be off by ~100 % to be seen: under load the iron current V/Rf is ~1 A out of 5–40 A and is in phase with the much larger active current. At no-load the rotor branch is open and P0 − 3·R1·I0² − p_mec is the iron loss itself (+1 % Rf → −0,99 % P0), so Rf is set there. Xm and X become correlated at light load (ρ ≈ 0,95 for g = 0,004…0,006, 0,64 for g = 0,02…0,40): near g = 0 the rotor branch is almost purely R'2/g and both reactances only add reactive current. Load points should therefore go towards breakdown. |
| A14 | identification | Load fit residuals are normalised per reading (`normalise="point"`, default): (model − measured)/|measured|. Confidence intervals: linearised, Cov = s²·(JᵀJ)⁻¹, Student t; they exclude the uncertainty of the fixed R1 and Rf | 95 % | The demo noise is relative to each reading (2,5 % of the value), so each residual is divided by its reading. Checked by 500 noisy draws: coverage Xm 96,4 %, X 95,8 %, R'2 94,0 % per reading, against 97,0 / 90,8 / 94,6 % with `normalise="quantity"` (still available). The X shortfall under "quantity" came from the noise model (relative noise is larger at high load, where X is decided), not from Rf (91,0 % with the true Rf) nor the robust loss (90,4 % with a linear loss). Real TP3 data should later be weighted by the accuracy class of each instrument (ammeter, wattmeter, torquemeter), i.e. σ = class × full scale, not × reading. |
| A15 | identification | Classical locked-rotor bias on TD5 Ex 1 | R'2 −13,1 %, X −7,7 % | Present even without noise: the magnetising branch is neglected at g = 1, although Xm/X ≈ 11,5 only (46 Ω / 4 Ω), so Zm in parallel lowers the measured Zcc. |
| A16 | drive | Drive settings of tab 2 are user inputs; the defaults are démo values: i_max = 20 A, boost = 0 V, margin = 80 % of the breakdown torque, f_max = 100 Hz | 20 A, 0 V, 0,8, 100 Hz | Inverter ratings are not given by TD5/TP3. 20 A ≈ 1,4 × the TD5 Ex 1 current at its rated slip (14,05 A at g = 0,1), so the current limit binds and all three zones appear; with i_max ≥ 26 A the 80 % breakdown limit binds first and the constant-power zone vanishes (tested). |
| A17 | drive | Envelope computed per speed: at each Ω the drive picks the rotor frequency f_r that maximises the torque under I1 ≤ i_max, T ≤ margin·T_bd(f) and f ≤ f_max; torque is electromagnetic; stator frequency ≥ f_min = fn/100; the envelope ends where f = f_max; Rf stays constant with f in the envelope | f_min = 0,5 Hz | A per-frequency envelope (each frequency's limit point placed at its own speed) underestimates the torque when the breakdown limit binds: on TD5 (60 A, margin 1) it gave 74,7 N·m at 1200 tr/min, while 50 Hz at g = 0,2 already gives 87,1 N·m (I1 = 24,5 A) and the per-speed maximum is 92,3 N·m (tested against a brute-force scan). With the default 20 A / 80 % the two agree within one grid step, because the current or margin limit binds. f_min avoids the overflux of the linear magnetising branch at f → 0 with boost. Mechanical losses are subtracted in core.losses; the frequency-dependent Rf of A19 is applied in the losses map only. |
| A18 | drive | Zone boundaries read from the curve: base speed = last point before d ln T/d ln Ω < −0,5; end of constant power = first point above base where the breakdown limits (margin·T_bd, or the speed's own torque peak) | −0,5 | Halfway between the constant-torque (n = 0) and constant-power (n = −1) laws. With R1 = 0, T_bd·Ω_s² is constant above fn (exact), but the available torque × Ω_rotor² is not (29 % spread on TD5): its slip falls from about 0,25 to 0,12 across field weakening, because TD5's rotor is very resistive (g_c = 0,5). Zone 3 therefore has n ≈ −1,7, not −2. |
| A19 | losses | Iron losses follow Steinmetz, P_fe ∝ f^β·B² with B ∝ E/f, i.e. P_fe = 3·E²/Rf·(f/fn)^(β−2), implemented as Rf(f) = Rf·(f/fn)^(2−β) inside the circuit | β = 1,5 | TP3 gives Rf at 50 Hz only, so β cannot be identified: 1,5 lies between hysteresis (β = 1) and eddy currents (β = 2), typical of laminations at 50–100 Hz. At fn the map equals the circuit for any β (tested). Literally multiplying 3·V²/Rf by (f/fn)^β would count the frequency twice, since V ∝ f under V/f. |
| A20 | losses | Mechanical losses P_mec(Ω) = a·Ω + b·Ω², equal to the identified p_mec at Ω_0 = 2π·fn/p; friction (a·Ω) share s of p_mec(Ω_0) | s = 0,5 | The no-load separation of tab 1 gives p_mec at one speed only; the split between bearing friction and windage needs a run-down test. 0,5 is a neutral split, editable in tab 3. TD5 Ex 1: negligible (p_mec = 0). |
| A21 | losses | Inverter losses P_inv = P_0 + k·P_motor | P_0 = 50 W, k = 2 % (démo) | No inverter data in TD5/TP3; order of magnitude of a small IGBT inverter (≈ 97–98 % at rated load). Labelled démo in tab 3 and editable. |

| A23 | vehicle | Rated power for the power-to-mass ratio = maximum shaft power along the drive envelope; G_max = 0,95·r·Ω_max/v_max (top speed inside the envelope), then the G below G_max that maximises the worst-case torque margin over the cycle | 0,95 | WLTP uses the maximum power of an electric vehicle. | A28 | thermal | Insulation life by the 10 K (Montsinger) rule with L_ref = 20 000 h at the class temperature (class F: 155 °C). Under a varying winding temperature the ageing rates add (Miner's rule): L = L_ref / mean(2^((T_w − T_class)/10)) over the samples of the last simulated cycle; at constant T this is L_ref·2^((T_class − T)/10). Shown as an order of magnitude | 20 000 h | IEC 60085 classes are defined around 20 000 h. The rule is convex in T, so the life at the mean temperature is optimistic: 50 % of the time at 60 °C and 50 % at 160 °C gives 2,8·10⁴ h, against 4,5·10⁵ h at the mean 110 °C (tested). Order of magnitude only: the hot spot is hotter than the winding average, and far below the class temperature the extrapolation is meaningless. `steady_state` raises « emballement thermique » when the copper losses grow with T faster than the network removes them (p_w/(234,5 + T_ref) ≥ 1/(R_ws + R_sa)). |
| A29 | winding | Conductors of a slot concentrated at the slot centre (zero slot opening); three-phase only (m = 3); belts A+ C− B+ A− C+ B−, phase currents i_k = I·cos(ωt − 2πk/3); kd·kp used for odd harmonics, even harmonics zero | — | Course model of the stepped MMF. With concentrated conductors the ν-th harmonic is exactly ∝ kw_ν/ν (the FFT agrees within 0,005 %). A+ and A− are one pole pitch apart with opposite signs, so the MMF has half-wave symmetry: even orders vanish. One phase keeps its triplen harmonics; they cancel in the three-phase field, whose fundamental then has a constant amplitude and turns at 2πf/p (to machine precision). Slot opening and saturation would add slot harmonics, not modelled. |

| A33 | circuit | **Circuit magnétique linéaire**: Xm (and X) are constant at a given frequency, scaled only by f/fn; no saturation. The air-gap flux is measured by E/f, E = |V − R1·I1| the voltage across the magnetising branch (= V in the terminals layout), relative to E_n/fn with E_n = E at the rated no-load point. With a V/f boost V0 > 0 the flux exceeds rated at low speed, where a real machine saturates (its magnetising current rises much faster and the torque gain is smaller than the model shows). Along the envelope the flux is taken as the worse of the full-torque point and the no-load point (g = 0) at the same V and f | — | TP3 gives one no-load point near Vn, so no magnetisation curve is identified. At full torque the R1·I1 drop absorbs the boost (E/f ≤ 0,97 × rated on the identified démo motor even with 10 V), but at light load the same V and f over-flux (1,16 × rated with 7,3 V): hence the worse of the two. Without boost E/f ≤ E_n/fn at every speed (tested). Tab 2 warns with `Envelope.max_flux_ratio` whenever it exceeds 1. Tab 7 adds the constraint max E/f ≤ 1,10·En/fn (`optimise.FLUX_LIMIT`): a 10 % over-flux is about where a typical induction motor leaves the linear part of its magnetisation curve; above it the linear model is no longer trusted. |
